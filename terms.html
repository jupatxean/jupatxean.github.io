3. What JupaFit is, and what it is not

JupaFit estimates the nutrition of food, builds training plans from what you tell us, and answers questions through an AI coach.

It is not medical advice

This matters more than anything else on this page.

JupaFit is not a doctor, a dietitian, or a physiotherapist, and nothing in it is medical advice. The calorie and protein targets are arithmetic from the height, weight, age and goal you typed in. The training plan is a programme, not a prescription. The coach is a language model.

  • Talk to a doctor before starting if you have any medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are recovering from injury or surgery, or take medication that affects appetite, weight, blood sugar or heart rate.
  • Stop and get help if something hurts. Pain in a joint, chest pain, dizziness, or feeling faint are reasons to stop exercising and speak to a professional — not reasons to push through.
  • If tracking food is making things worse, stop tracking. Calorie counting helps some people and harms others. If logging is making you anxious about eating, making you restrict, or taking up more of your day than it is giving back, the app is not helping you and you should talk to someone about it. That is a real thing that happens to real people using apps like this one, and no target on a screen is worth it.

The AI features are estimates

The meal scanner reads one photograph. It cannot see what is under the surface, cannot weigh anything, and does not know what went into the cooking. Its numbers are estimates and some of them will be wrong — sometimes by a lot. Treat them as a rough guide, not a measurement, and correct them when you know better.

The coach can be confidently wrong in the way language models are. Do not act on anything it says about a medical symptom, a medication, or an injury without checking with a person qualified to answer.

You are responsible for what you do with any of it.
